About This Dashboard

Understanding How America Grows, Moves & Builds Roads

Population growth, vehicle travel, and road network capacity are three of the most fundamental indicators of how a society expands and evolves. As states grow — or shrink — their transportation systems must respond. Yet the relationships among how many people live somewhere, how much those people drive, and how much road capacity exists are far from simple. They vary by geography, by era, and by economic circumstance.

This dashboard brings together 16 years of annual data — from 2010 through 2025 — on population estimates, vehicle miles traveled (VMT), and lane miles for all 50 U.S. states and Washington D.C. It is designed to help researchers, planners, policymakers, and curious citizens explore how these measures have changed over time, how they compare across states, and where driving and road-building have kept pace with — or diverged from — population growth.

The period covered spans a remarkable range of conditions: the slow recovery from the 2008 financial crisis, a sustained economic expansion through the mid-2010s, the sharp COVID-19 disruption of 2020 that caused a historic single-year plunge in VMT, and the subsequent rebound through 2025. Lane miles data is available through 2024 from FHWA. Together, these datasets tell a rich and nuanced story about mobility and capacity in America.

How to Use This Dashboard
A quick guide to getting the most from the interactive features.
Step 1
Choose your year range
Every analytical tab has dual sliders letting you set a custom start and end year from 2010 to 2025. All charts and maps update instantly.
Step 2
Select geographies
In Tabs 1 and 2, add up to 3 states, D.C., or the U.S. total to compare their trends simultaneously on the same chart.
Step 3
Choose your metrics
Use the Chart dropdown in Tabs 1 and 2 to choose which pair of variables to display. In Tabs 2 and 3, use checkboxes to toggle individual metrics on and off in the indexed and comparison charts.
Step 4
Explore the maps & hover for details
All maps support scroll-to-zoom, click-and-drag panning, and double-click zoom. Switch between map metrics using the buttons above each map. Hover over any state, bar, or scatter dot for a detailed tooltip with precise values.
2020 COVID Note
All data includes the year 2020. VMT dropped approximately 13% nationally due to COVID-19 pandemic travel restrictions while population remained relatively stable. This creates a visible V-shaped dip in VMT trend lines for that year across nearly every state.

This entire dashboard — from data assembly to interactive visualization — was conceived and constructed through a collaborative conversation with Claude (Anthropic's AI assistant). Claude retrieved population data from the U.S. Census Bureau, VMT data from the FHWA Highway Statistics VM-2 table, and lane miles data from the FHWA HM-60 table (fetching each year's data directly from the FHWA website), compiled all three into structured Excel spreadsheets, and then designed and coded this full interactive HTML dashboard with D3.js maps, Chart.js visualizations, multi-geography selectors, indexed trend charts, zoom-and-pan maps, state comparison tools, and scatter plot quadrant analysis. All analytical tabs, choropleth maps, per capita calculations, lane miles metrics, and VMT/lane mile utilization analysis were generated entirely through natural language dialogue — no manual coding required.
About This Dashboard
America in Motion is an interactive data dashboard exploring 16 years of population change, vehicle miles traveled (VMT), and road network capacity (lane miles) across all 50 U.S. states and Washington D.C., from 2010 through 2025. Population and VMT data run through 2025; lane miles data is available through 2024 from the FHWA Highway Statistics series. The dashboard is designed to help researchers, planners, policymakers, and curious citizens explore national and state-level trends, compare geographies, and understand how population growth, driving, and road capacity have diverged or converged over time.
What's Inside
Tab 1 — Trends & Indexed
Choose from three dual-axis chart pairs: Population vs VMT, Population vs Lane Miles, or Lane Miles vs VMT. The indexed view shows Population, VMT, and Lane Miles all normalized to 1.0. Interactive map with three metric options and year slider.
Tab 2 — Per Capita
Six dual-axis chart combinations covering Population, VMT/capita, Lane Miles/capita, and VMT/Lane Mile. Indexed chart with four toggleable metrics. Maps color states by VMT/capita, Population, Lane Miles/capita, or VMT/Lane Mile.
Tab 3 — State Comparison
All-state bar chart with six metrics: Population, VMT, VMT/capita, Lane Miles, Lane Miles/capita, and VMT/Lane Mile. U.S. total shown as a benchmark. Sortable ascending/descending, filterable, with six choropleth map options.
Tab 4 — Scatter Plot
Three scatter plot options: Pop % vs VMT %, Pop % vs Lane Miles %, and Lane Miles % vs VMT %. States color-coded into four median-based quadrants (Green/Blue/Orange/Red). Matching choropleth map updates with each scatter selection.

Data Source 3
Lane Miles of Public Roads
FHWA Highway Statistics — Table HM-60
Total lane miles of public roads by functional system for all 50 states and D.C. From the FHWA Highway Statistics series, Table HM-60 (Functional System Lane-Length). Data for each year retrieved directly from the FHWA annual statistics pages. Lane miles represent the total length of all travel lanes on public roads. Coverage: 50 states + D.C. + U.S. total
Years: 2010–2024 (2025 not yet published by FHWA)
Units: Lane miles (total, all functional classes)
Publisher: Federal Highway Administration

Data Notes
Population figures are July 1 annual estimates and may be revised by the Census Bureau in subsequent vintages. VMT data reflects total annual travel on all public roads and streets. Lane miles data is from FHWA HM-60 and covers 2010–2024; the 2025 edition has not yet been published. Lane mile totals can shift year-over-year due to reclassification of roads between functional systems, changes in urban area boundaries (particularly after the 2010 Census), and HPMS data revisions — occasional year-over-year decreases are real and not data errors. The year 2020 reflects significant COVID-19 pandemic effects — VMT dropped approximately 13% nationally while population and lane miles remained relatively stable. FHWA periodically revises prior-year estimates; values may differ slightly from older publications.
